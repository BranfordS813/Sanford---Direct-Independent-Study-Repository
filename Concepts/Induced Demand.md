
Induced demand is a positive feedback loop related to road widening when trying to counter road congestion. When roads are congested, traffic engineers will often widen the road by adding more lands and even increasing speed limits at times. This may, in the short term, result in reduced volumes; however, over time in the long run, more drivers who haven't used the expanded road will begin to travel on it more. In addition, with wider road and lane expansions leading to reduced traffic, developers will move in and construct new places around the road, like housing, business, and commerce. As a result (especially with housing development resulting in more people having to drive), more and more people are drive the newly expanded road until the road, once again becomes congested, which incentivizes traffic engineers and planners to 'add one more lane' and expand the road but only for the road to be used more and get congested again, contributing to the positive feedback loop. The demand for the road expansion is 'induced' due to the expanded road leading to more traffic. The action of 'adding one more lane' leads back to the condition of more congested traffic.
